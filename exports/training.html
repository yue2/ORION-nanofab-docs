Training Overview

Training is two 3-hour sessions followed by a 30-minute competency test.

  • Safety Training Alarm recognition, cryogenic/high-voltage hazards and escalation.
  • Session 1 Principles, checks, ZEN, loading, alignment and imaging.
  • Session 2 Practice, flood gun and patterning.
  • Competency Test 30-minute assessment; extra training if required.

Safety Training

Trainees must finish facility fire safety induction before requesting tool trainings.

Facility facts every trainee must know:

  1. O₂ monitoring — the O₂ monitor sits on the door frame outside the HIM room; normal reading is 20–21%. If an N₂ leak drops O₂ below 20%, evacuate personnel immediately (HIM training, p. 1; see O₂/N₂ Safety).
  2. Fire alarm and gas suppression — the HIM lab uses a gas-based fire-suppression system. When the fire alarm sounds, a suppression gas will be released after a 60-second delay. Do not attempt to fight the fire — evacuate immediately to the designated fire assembly point (HIM training, p. 1; see Fire & Gas Suppression).
  3. Corridor extinguisher — the extinguisher mounted on the corridor wall is for small office-area fires only, outside the HIM room; it is not for suppressing a fire in the HIM lab.

The trainer must also cover, with reference to the Safety and Superuser pages:

  • LN₂ / cryogenic hazards and the Dewar labels (see LN₂ System; ZEISS manual, printed p. 11 / PDF p. 16)
  • Electrical / high-voltage labels and the EMO (emergency mains off) switches (see High Voltage; ZEISS manual, printed p. 13 / PDF p. 18)
  • Vacuum and chamber hazards (see Vacuum Chamber)
  • The difference between user stop/escalation ("stop and contact a Superuser") and Superuser recovery actions (see Emergency and the User Guide "Warning Signals / When to Stop")

Training Session 1

Agenda for the first hands-on session. Prereading: Safety Training.

  1. Basic microscopy and environmental stability — why the HIM needs vacuum, stable gun temperature, and acoustic/mechanical isolation:
  • Vacuum regions: gun/column vs. chamber; base-pressure expectations (see Reference: Vacuum System).
  • Gun temperature and the LN₂ Dewar; why acoustic and table stability matter for imaging (see LN₂ System).
  1. Tool components and HIM working principle — GFIS source, trimer, extraction/accelerating column, Lens 2, apertures, deflectors, ET detector, stage; He vs. Ne operation (see System Overview, GFIS Controls).
  2. Pre-use checks — complete before each session:
  • Review previous log entries and alerts.
  • Record gun, mid-column and chamber base pressures; gun temperature and Dewar bottom pressure; gas/CDA and LN₂ status.
  • Verify trimer and communications when appropriate (see Routine Checks and Before You Start).
  1. ZEN GUI and status bars — Operation Center, Vacuum Dashboard, Sample Management, GFIS/Gas Box panels, Dewar Control; how to read state indicators and alarms.
  2. Sample holder, mounting, height, tilt/rotation, loading/unloading — holder handling with clean gloves; sample mounting and height limits (confirm the reconciled 6 mm limit with the Tool Owner before teaching); stage tilt −5° to +54° and rotation; the full load/unload sequence and its interlocks (see Sample Loading & Unloading).
  3. Hands-on transfer into the main chamber — supervised practice of: Exchange → Transfer → rod insertion and secure coupling → visual confirmation via chamber camera that the holder is seated → full retraction with rod-retracted interlock verified. Emphasize the two critical failure modes: never retract before confirming the holder is seated/secure, and never force a tilted rod (see Sample Loading & Unloading).
  • Trainer reminder: dropping the sample stage/holder by mishandling the transfer rod forces a main-chamber vent and counts as one unauthorized operation toward the two-strikes access policy — emphasize this before hands-on practice (it is a common mishap).
  • Trainer reminder: No volatile substance is allowed in this tool — advise users during training, especially about organic samples. Loading an inappropriate volatile/organic sample counts as one unapproved operation
  1. Beam alignment, astigmatism correction, focus and imaging practice — practice on the standard Au sample: reach rough focus (Lens 2), confirm aperture/gas/landing-energy set points, correct astigmatism with the stigmator knobs, and iterate focus/wobble/gun-shift. Verify trimer status before serious imaging (see Starting a Session, Imaging).

End-of-session check: trainee can demonstrate items 3–6; formal assessment is tracked in the Competency Test.

Training Session 2

Second hands-on session. Prerequisites: Session 1 skills (loading/unloading, focus, alignment) and the Safety Training briefing. The trainee may bring their own sample; it must meet the sample height and mounting rules from Session 1.

  1. Hands-on repetition of loading/unloading — supervised repeat of the Session-1 transfer sequence, emphasizing the interlocks and failure modes: never retract the rod before confirming the holder is seated/secure; never force a tilted rod (see Sample Loading & Unloading).
  2. Practice tuning focus on the standard Au sample — reach rough focus with Lens 2, correct astigmatism, and iterate focus/wobble/gun-shift until the image is stable (see Starting a Session, Imaging).
  3. Practice locating and focusing on the trainee's own sample — navigate to saved stage positions, find the region of interest, and set focus/contrast/dwell for good imaging.
  4. Flood-gun use on an standard insulating (non-conducting) sample — before powering the flood gun, confirm the ET detector grid is not shorted (ZEISS §4.9, printed pp. 88–89 / PDF pp. 93–94). Practice line-mode neutralization, flood time, and re-checking astigmatism afterward (see Imaging — Using flood gun).
  5. Patterning using ZEN — draw a simple shape in the Patterning tab, set dose and verify expected beam current, start/stop the pattern and check the result (see Patterning — ZEN patterning).
  6. Patterning using NPVE — verify the server/communication connection, grab a reference image, draw or import a pattern, define a stop condition, and run it (see Patterning — NPVE; advanced details in the NPVE User Guide).
  7. Review and issue — review the trainee's training notes and logbook together, answer open questions, and issue the User Guide: point the trainee to the User Guide section (starting page) and the printable export exports/user-guide.html.

End-of-session check: trainee can load/unload, focus, use the flood gun on an insulating sample, and run a basic ZEN and NPVE pattern unassisted; formal assessment is tracked in the Competency Test.

Competency Test

Duration: ~30 minutes. Sample: standard gold (Au) sample for all imaging items. Open book: the User Guide may be brought along and consulted during the test. Pass rule: the trainee must demonstrate every item below independently (minimal prompting). If the trainee does not qualify, schedule additional training before granting independent access.

The assessor marks each item Pass/Fail:

1. Safety precautions

  • [ ] Identifies the key hazards and precautions from the safety briefing: O₂ monitoring (evacuate below 20%), fire alarm / gas-suppression response (abort vs. evacuate), LN₂/cryogenic handling, high-voltage/EMO, and chamber/detector collision risks (see Safety Training, Warning Signals / When to Stop).
  • [ ] States the "stop and contact a Superuser" conditions and confirms they will not attempt them alone (e.g., chamber venting, HV work, pump or source recovery).

2. Load / unload samples

  • [ ] Loads and unloads a sample holder through the full sequence: Exchange → Stage Ready → Transfer → rod coupling and seating verification → retraction with the rod-retracted interlock confirmed (see Sample Loading & Unloading).
  • [ ] Demonstrates the two critical cautions: never retract the rod before confirming the holder is secured/seated, and never force a tilted rod.

3. Turn on the He beam

  • [ ] Selects the standard Au sample, powers on the ET detector, and confirms Normal imaging mode.
  • [ ] Sets the He aperture/gas, landing energy, and spot control; ramps up Lens 2 with the leakage-current check (≤0.5 µA) (see Starting a Session).

4. Focus on the Au sample

  • [ ] Achieves a focused image on the standard Au sample, correcting astigmatism and beam shift/wobble as needed (see Starting a Session, Imaging).

5. Take an image and save it to the folder

  • [ ] Sets scan parameters, grabs an image, and saves it to the designated data location (see Imaging — Imaging your sample).

6. Turn off the beam

  • [ ] Shuts down correctly: gas Off / GFIS Standby, Lens 2 ramped down and powered off (below 100 V), ET detector (and flood gun, if used) powered down; confirms the vacuum state (see Ending a Session).

7. Simple patterning operation (ZEN)

  • [ ] Demonstrates a simple pattern with the ZEN Patterning tab: draws a shape, sets the dose and verifies the expected beam current, runs and stops the pattern (see Patterning — ZEN patterning).

8. NPVE patterning (required only if the trainee will use NPVE for their future experiments)

  • [ ] Verifies the server/communication connection, grabs a reference image, draws or imports a simple pattern, defines a stop condition, and runs it (see Patterning — NPVE; advanced details in the NPVE User Guide).

After all items pass, the trainee may receive independent (non-Superuser) tool access. Record the result, date, and inform lab managers for record keeping and door access.
